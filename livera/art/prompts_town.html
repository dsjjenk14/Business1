Style sheet (already inside every prompt) 
LIVÉRA town sprite. Isometric 2:1 (30°) view from the south-west, warm golden-hour light from the west with a soft champagne glow, warm amber light in the windows, clean photoreal render, no outlines, no sky. The Livéra palette: ivory and sand stone, champagne-gold trim and serif lettering, deep emerald green accents (awnings, doors, sign plates), warm brick only where named, charcoal glass with thin gold edges, lush rounded sage-green trees with a few tiny warm lights, pale cream paving. Transparent background, no ground shadow outside the footprint, no people, no cars, no text except the sign named below. Scale: a door is 1 unit tall; a one-storey shop is about 3 units.
  • View. Isometric 2:1 (a 30° dimetric view), camera from the south-west, so the front and the left side of a building are visible. The same angle for every sprite, or they will not sit together on the map.
  • Light. Warm golden-hour light from the west, the same in every sprite: a soft champagne glow on the stone, warm amber light in the windows, shadows soft and kept inside the footprint. The sky itself is left out (it cannot be shared between tiles); the map draws the sky and moves it through the day.
  • Palette (art/STYLE.md, sampled from the owner's reference art). Ivory and sand stone (#f7ecd6, #f0dec2, #d9c9a9), champagne gold for trim and lettering (#dabb85), deep emerald for awnings, doors and sign plates (#0b2b23), sage and olive planting (#939c87, #7a8161), warm brick (#ad734e) only where a prompt names brick, charcoal glass with thin gold edges for towers and offices, pale cream paving.
  • Motifs. The gold lotus mark on Livéra's own things (transit, civic signs where a prompt asks); rounded lush trees with a few tiny warm lights; brass lamps; no neon except the nightclub.
  • Sprite. "Transparent background, no ground shadow outside the footprint": the image model supports transparency. One building per image. Nothing else in frame: no street, no neighbours, no sky.
  • No people, no cars. The town's population is abstract numbers and driving does not exist. The only vehicle is the Line 1 bus, which has its own prompts.
  • Signage. The real name, at most two words, in gold serif capitals. Image models misspell longer text. If an accent (LIVÉRA, LUMIÈRE, ÉCLAT) comes out wrong, generate again or accept the plain letters and say so in the manifest notes.
  • Size. 1024×1024 for small places, tiles and the bus front and rear; 1536×1024 for long buildings and the bus side; 1024×1536 (portrait) for the two tall things, the Lexington tower and the crane.
  • Scale rule. A door is 1 unit tall and a one-storey shop is about 3 units, so a four-storey block is about 12 units and the Lexington is far taller. Keep doors the same size everywhere.
  • Honesty. Draw only what the data says. Where a prompt adds a detail that is not in the data it is marked "(art direction)" in the facts. Station 1 Fire & Rescue is a placeholder landmark. The Underpass sells illegal things after dark: draw the corner, never the trade.

What these prompts are not

  • The street layout, building shapes, the river and the parks' outlines are the sketch client's (web/client/layout.js), not game data. The data gives each place a door coordinate, a type, a district, a description, hours and access, and that is what these prompts are built from.
  • No data file gives the river a shape. Six descriptions mention it and the Riverside district is named after it, and the sketch draws it along the east edge of the map. The river-edge tile is offered on that basis.
  • Apartment and condo units that share a door are one building in the game's map, so they get one prompt and one file, named by the id prefix the client uses (lexington, maple_court, riverside_lofts, lakeview).
  • Construction sites get two prompts, the bare site and the framed stage, because the client draws a rising frame and a crane while a project is active. Nothing in the data says what a half-built building looks like; the framed stage is art direction.
  • art/places/body_balance.jpg is an earlier photograph of the gym; the map uses the isometric sprite drawn from it (body_balance.webp), and the manifest keeps the photograph as its photo.
  • When a generated picture gets a fact wrong, say so in art/manifest.json under notes, as the other art does. Nobody should mistake a picture for the game's state.
